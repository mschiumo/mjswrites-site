In a startling turn of events, Jensen Huang was reinstated as CEO of Nvidia today by unanimous decision of the board. The consensus to bring back the disgraced founder came after direct intervention from President Trump and senior White House officials. Within minutes, $NVDA jumped over 30%, adding nearly a trillion dollars to its market cap by market close, the largest single-day gain of any stock in history. Passionate fans of Jensen and holders of the chipmaker’s stock rejoiced, with many finding renewed optimism about the progress of AI, as well as the chance for a continuation of the bull market. However, I am not certain that this is truly the time to celebrate. Today’s decision does not come without consequence, and a momentary sigh of relief may be just that — momentary.

## The Visible Hand

<figure>
  <img src="/images/adam-smith-invisible-hand.avif" alt="Adam Smith and his ‘Invisible Hand’" />
  <figcaption>Adam Smith and his ‘Invisible Hand’</figcaption>
</figure>

Renowned Scottish economist Adam Smith [coined the term ‘invisible hand’](https://www.adamsmithworks.org/documents/adam-smith-peter-foster-invisible-hand) to argue that self-interest in free markets ultimately benefits society at large. Yet, what we are seeing in the United States is quite the opposite of a ‘free market,’ and it seems quite clear who benefits from this recent turn of events. In this case, it is the government’s hand that has intervened, not only by [overturning a decision by the board of a publicly traded company](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-jensen-huang-china-national-security-chips-3026bcbe04ab57b844884382e583422a), but by dismissing what could be [one of the largest threats to US national security in modern history](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/22/us/politics/alice-huang-china-spy-agency-nvidia.html).

One of the major tenets of the massive push to accelerate AI development in the United States has been the argument that this is the 21st century’s equivalent of the nuclear arms race. Therefore, it stands to reason that, if artificial intelligence is truly as dangerous as a nuclear weapon, the mishandling of it, especially with regard to an adversary like China, should be considered traitorous. What’s more, Jensen was reinstated *before* the US government had the chance to launch a full-scale investigation into the matter. Not only does this show a severe fracture in protocol for handling matters of such gravity as they pertain to national security, it seems to carry with it a sense of desperation.

Last week, amid the significant slide in the market following the breaking of Jensen’s multiple scandals, [Anthropic launched its IPO](/blog/yes-anthropics-ipo-matters) in what can only be described as a suboptimal market environment. Many, including me, feel that the reinstatement of Jensen was a last-ditch attempt to restore investor confidence in order to prop up equity markets, which are extraordinarily concentrated in the ‘AI trade.’ One reason could be that investors, namely the massive corporations and private creditors that have dumped trillions into the AI industry over the past few years, needed to deleverage.

Due to the fact that the recent market selloff was so precipitous, it can be surmised that many of these investors, as well as hedge funds and other money managers, were being margin-called, leading to sharp drops in price ($NVDA fell over 40% from its record high in a matter of weeks). This could likewise be the reason that precious metals, like gold, have sold off sharply, as they are often among the most liquid investments in the event of a crisis.

This is but one of countless instances of Trump and his administration [unapologetically manipulating markets](https://www.fundssociety.com/en/news/markets/the-surprises-after-one-year-of-liberation-day-neither-stock-market-crash-nor-recession/), and a prime example of the dangers of having a president with a direct financial interest in the success of a company. Although we do not have an exact number in terms of shares that Trump owns, we do know that Nvidia is among his three largest holdings, and [could be worth upwards of $60 million](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/11/16/trumps-stake-nvidia-grew-much-62-million-before-scandal-broke-out/), according to the Washington Post. Moreover, Jensen has been a close advisor to Trump on matters concerning AI development, even traveling with the President to China for discussions with Xi Jinping back in May. Trump’s affection for Jensen has been on full display, with the President lauding him as a ‘genius’ and a ‘good person,’ even as [news of additional sexual affairs with direct reports](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/11/technology/jensen-huang-nvidia-affair.html) within Nvidia came to light. Ultimately, the President’s judgment is clouded by both greed and sentimentality, and the [dismissal of Jensen as a potential risk to national security](https://virginiabusiness.com/trump-assured-nvidia-jensen-huang-no-national-security-risk/) is not only premature, but dangerous.

## Walking the Tightrope

<figure>
  <img src="/images/jensen-trump-xi-summit.webp" alt="Jensen, Trump, and Xi at an AI summit in China, May 2026" />
  <figcaption>Jensen, Trump, and Xi at an AI summit in China, May 2026</figcaption>
</figure>

As jaw-dropping as Jensen’s return to the helm of what is arguably the most influential company on the planet has been, we are certainly not out of the woods yet. I am all but certain that there will be many more salacious details that emerge in his coming testimony, as well as evidence of Trump’s mishandling of this alleged breach, about which the administration purportedly [received briefings from US intelligence over a year ago](https://www.ms.now/opinion/jensen-huang-nvidia-howard-lutnick-donald-trump). Moreover, Commerce Secretary and fervent Trump ally Howard Lutnick is under renewed scrutiny for his failure to enforce export bans on Nvidia’s proprietary chips to China, further entrenching the administration in what could prove to be an inextricable situation. As the ‘Blue Wave’ of fresh-faced Democrats is set to take office in just a few months, this could be grounds for a third Trump impeachment.

More than that, the threat that China poses to the United States and its bet on AI has only grown over time. As it stands, [many Chinese AI models have matched or exceeded the performance of their American counterparts](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/china-us-ai-race-hugging-face-models.html), and most are open-weight, meaning that anyone can view, manipulate, and improve upon the weights. Beyond this, Chinese models are considerably cheaper than those offered by US frontier labs, like Anthropic and OpenAI, and there is already significant evidence that [companies are choosing them over America’s current offerings](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/companies-turn-to-chinese-ai-models-to-cut-costs/). Finally, China has positioned itself as a leader in energy infrastructure, which is robust, rapidly growing, and increasingly built on renewables. This is in contrast to the United States, where the energy grid is outdated and [incapable of supporting the growing demand that comes with AI development](https://woods.stanford.edu/news/warning-ai-era-why-americas-energy-infrastructure-isnt-ready-whats-coming). Alas, you can have the best models in the world, but if you cannot run them due to a lack of power, then they are as good as useless.

The largest advantage that the US does have over China is access to compute, which is the primary reason that Nvidia is such a powerhouse in the industry — its chips are the best currently available on the market (although AMD looks intent on closing the gap). Put simply, if China were able to get its hands on these chips and learn to manufacture them domestically, it would have a definitive advantage. This is precisely why Jensen’s alleged wrongdoing could prove to be the ceding of dominance to China in the AI race, and why it demands the utmost caution.

On a final note, I would like to point out that Nvidia’s chips are almost exclusively produced and packaged in Taiwan, which finds itself in quite the precarious geopolitical situation with regard to China. This is a significant risk to the broader supply chain, as China [has been less than subtle in its dealings with the island nation](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/nov/18/inside-ring-china-advances-undersea-threat-targeting-taiwan/) in recent months. With the US military already embedded in a forever war with Iran, it is not out of the realm of possibility that, should the AI race heat up to the point of no return, China could exercise its military might to cut off this crucial advantage of the United States as the two nations race for supremacy on the world stage.

So, although Jensen, along with his trademark leather jacket, seems to be here to stay, the question is — for how much longer?
