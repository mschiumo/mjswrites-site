There is something a bit ironic about the perception around the ‘American’ identity right now - the reason that it is so beloved is the same reason that it is at the center of such a heated debate. Simply put, it looks different to everyone, and that is *precisely* the point.

I recently had a friend visit me in New York, her first time ever on American soil. Naturally, she wanted the ‘American’ experience. The question quickly became - what is truly *American*? Do we really have a definitive ‘cuisine’? Is there one thing that every single American enjoys doing? Is there one tradition or special occasion that is celebrated by all those who salute the Stars and Stripes? Of course not! However, that is where I believe the beauty of this country lies.

The United States was built on immigration. My ancestors left Europe to stake their claim in this new Land of Opportunity in the early 20th century, and were eager to assimilate and begin their new lives in this exciting world of dreams. Still, those who came from Italy drank wine and celebrated the San Gennaro Festival on Mott Street, and gathered around dining tables laden with meatballs and ziti to gossip away with their loved ones on holidays. My grandfather from Slovakia still taught us his favorite words in his native language, and impressed upon us the importance of the simple traditions that had defined his childhood. My grandmother from Scotland cried whenever the song *I’ll Be Home for Christmas* came on the radio around the holidays, because it reminded her of all the time that had passed since she had been in her own homeland. The point is that, despite the fact that they chose to forge new paths in the United States, it was still the essence of their homelands that defined them, an indelible part of their identity that would never wash away.

I find such joy in all of this. It makes life abundant with new things to learn. I find it particularly heartwarming to see how people simply light up when they have the chance to share a piece of their culture, whether it is a specialty dish, or just a word or two in their mother tongue. Beyond this, it is amazing that we are capable of living so harmoniously in the face of our differences, at least most of the time. Our diversity, it stands to reason, is a sign of strength. We each bring our own unique perspectives, knowledge, and talents to the table.

Thus, it is sad to see the rise of xenophobia and fear of the ‘other’ in America. Often, it is the result of propaganda that seeks to sow panic by peddling false narratives. The laughable part of it all is that no one could survive in the United States without relying on immigrants. In their absence, our society ceases to function. In their absence, this is no ‘America.’

As we prepare to celebrate Thanksgiving, perhaps one of the holidays that the majority of Americans celebrate, I want to reflect on the true meaning of it. We are told as children that it was the Native Americans who welcomed the European settlers, and they sat down to feast as a sort of peace offering. The history that followed, naturally, was sordid, bloody, and tragic. Yet, I want to focus in on the primary theme here - the welcoming of the ‘other.’ By its nature, Thanksgiving is an expression of gratitude for being accepted and treated with humanity and kindness. If there is one aspect that seems to unite us as a species, it is the act of dining together. We find ourselves freest in these moments, when we feel secure, fortunate, and among those we love.

I will be channeling this spirit in the coming week. I am forever grateful to my forebears, who took the ultimate leap of faith to provide me with the opportunities I have. I have unfathomable admiration for anyone who has chosen to leave behind everything that they know to try to make it here, and for the sheer number of sacrifices they must endure as a result. More than anything, I am steadfast in my belief that there is no singular portrait of what it means to be ‘American.’ We are a collection of people who want to see our nation prosper, and are willing to contribute in what ways we can.
